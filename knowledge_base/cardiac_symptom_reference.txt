ENTRY_ID: chest_pain_001
TOPIC: Chest Pain — Cardiac Red Flags
SOURCES:
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition, "Chest Pain" (Cardiology)
    https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/symptoms-of-cardiovascular-disorders/chest-pain
  - UCSF Hospital Handbook, "Diagnosis of Suspected ACS"
    https://hospitalhandbook.ucsf.edu/02-diagnosis-suspected-acs/02-diagnosis-suspected-acs
---
VERIFIED_CONTENT:
Red flag findings associated with dangerous causes of chest pain
include abnormal vital signs (tachycardia, bradycardia, tachypnea,
hypotension), signs of hypoperfusion (confusion, ashen color,
diaphoresis), shortness of breath, and new heart murmurs (Merck
Manual).

Features that make acute coronary syndrome (ACS) more likely
include: radiation to both arms, radiation to the left or right
arm, radiation to the neck or jaw, pain similar to prior ischemic
episodes, worsening with exertion, associated diaphoresis, or
associated dyspnea. Pain that is pleuritic or associated with
syncope makes ACS less likely, though these features alone cannot
reliably rule ACS in or out (UCSF Hospital Handbook).

RELEVANT_ECG_CHECK (per source):
New ECG changes such as ST elevation/depression or new left bundle
branch block are listed as red flags requiring urgent evaluation.

ENTRY_ID: syncope_001
TOPIC: Syncope — Cardiac Red Flags
SOURCE: Merck Manual Professional Edition, "Syncope" (Cardiology)
https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/symptoms-of-cardiovascular-disorders/syncope
---
VERIFIED_CONTENT:
Syncope with exertion suggests cardiac outflow obstruction or
exercise-induced arrhythmia; such patients sometimes also have
chest pain, palpitations, or both.

Syncope that begins and ends suddenly and spontaneously is typical
of cardiac causes, most commonly an arrhythmia. Syncope occurring
while lying down also suggests an arrhythmia, because vasovagal
and orthostatic mechanisms do not typically cause syncope in the
recumbent position.

Family history should note the presence, at a young age, of heart
disease or sudden death in any family member. A family history of
sudden unexpected death, exertional syncope, or unexplained
recurrent syncope or seizures is noted as important, since the
occasional life-threatening cause (e.g., tachyarrhythmia, heart
block) carries a risk of sudden death.

ENTRY_ID: palpitations_001
TOPIC: Palpitations — Associated Symptoms and Risk
SOURCE: Merck Manual Professional Edition, "Palpitations" (Cardiology)
https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/symptoms-of-cardiovascular-disorders/palpitations
---
VERIFIED_CONTENT:
Important associated symptoms to ask about alongside palpitations
include syncope, light-headedness, tunnel vision, dyspnea, and
chest pain.

Bradyarrhythmias, tachyarrhythmias, and heart blocks can be
unpredictable and may adversely affect cardiac output, causing
hypotension or death; ventricular tachycardia sometimes degenerates
into ventricular fibrillation.

Asking the patient to tap out the rate and cadence of the
palpitations (rather than relying on verbal description alone) can
help suggest a diagnosis — for example, an irregular "missed beat"
pattern suggests atrial or ventricular extrasystoles, while a rapid,
totally irregular pattern suggests atrial fibrillation.

ENTRY_ID: dyspnea_001
TOPIC: Dyspnea — Cardiac (Heart Failure) Pattern
SOURCES:
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition, "Chronic Heart Failure"
    https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/heart-failure/chronic-heart-failure
  - Merck Manual Consumer Version, "Shortness of Breath"
    https://www.merckmanuals.com/home/lung-and-airway-disorders/symptoms-of-lung-disorders/shortness-of-breath
---
VERIFIED_CONTENT:
In earlier or milder heart failure, dyspnea occurs with varying
degrees of exertion. As heart failure worsens, dyspnea can occur
during rest and at night, sometimes causing nocturnal cough.
Dyspnea occurring immediately or soon after lying flat, and
relieved promptly by sitting up (orthopnea), is common as heart
failure advances.

In paroxysmal nocturnal dyspnea, dyspnea awakens the patient several
hours after lying down and is relieved only after sitting up for
15–20 minutes; this is described as an extreme form of orthopnea
and a sign of severe heart failure.

ENTRY_ID: edema_001
TOPIC: Peripheral Edema — Cardiac (Heart Failure) Pattern
SOURCES:
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition, "Right Heart Failure and Cor
    Pulmonale"
    https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/heart-failure/right-heart-failure-and-cor-pulmonale
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition, "Chronic Heart Failure"
    https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/heart-failure/chronic-heart-failure
  - Merck Manual Professional Edition, "Edema" (Cardiology)
    https://www.merckmanuals.com/professional/cardiovascular-disorders/symptoms-of-cardiovascular-disorders/edema
---
VERIFIED_CONTENT:
In right ventricular failure, the most common symptoms are
peripheral edema and fatigue. Findings can include peripheral
edema, neck vein distention, and hepatomegaly. In severe heart
failure, peripheral edema can extend to the thighs or higher;
severe edema in multiple areas is termed anasarca.

Common chronic heart failure symptoms include exertional dyspnea,
exercise intolerance, and peripheral edema (particularly in the
ankles and feet).

For a patient presenting with edema, the review of systems should
include checking for exertional dyspnea, orthopnea, and paroxysmal
nocturnal dyspnea as possible signs pointing to a heart failure
cause; sudden onset of edema should trigger prompt evaluation.
